# TruVoice: the pre-formatter

How L&H TruVoice (`tv_enua.dll`) pre-formats the text it reads in its address, e-mail and spreadsheet contexts. TextData sends a text's segments in these contexts through the pre-formatter before it queues them ([engine.md](engine.md) §1.4), with their characters 128 and up made spaces but `’ £ ¥ © ®`. This spec covers the spreadsheet context: how the pre-formatter reads a text's lines and writes them (section 1) and what it makes of a line (section 2); how the address and e-mail contexts read a text and its e-mail header lines, join its lines and space its words (section 3); and where the port differs (section 4). The rest of the address and e-mail contexts' pre-formatter isn't ported yet, and the engine doesn't use section 3 yet.

**In short.**
- In the spreadsheet context, the text is read a line at a time, up to each line feed. A line ends in CR LF, and the text's last one, if no line feed ends it, in CR CR LF.
- A line of more than 720 characters is cut after a dot and a space, else after a space, else at 720 characters, and each part ends in CR CR LF. Lines are wrapped at 77 columns without line breaks: a word too long for a row gets a character made a space.
- A line with two slashes is a date: `12/25/1999` is `December 25, 1999`.
- A line with one slash has a fraction: `3 1/2` is `3 and 1 half`, `2/3` `2 thirds`, `1/16` `1 sixteenth`, `5/24` `5 20 fourths`, `1/100` `1 /100`.
- A dash at the start, or between characters below A, makes every dash of the line `minus`: `-5` is `minus 5`, `7 - 3` and `7-3` `7 minus 3`. In a line with both brackets, `(` is `minus` and `)` is dropped: `(12)` is `minus 12`.
- `E+` and `E-` after a number are exponents: `1.5E+10` is `1.5 times 10 to the 10`, `2E-3` `2 times 10 to the minus 3`.
- Escape sequences at a line's start are kept as they are.
- In the address and e-mail contexts, lines are joined into paragraphs, each followed by CR CR LF, and the text's end writes ` ` CR CR LF. A line under 39 characters gets `.  ` after it: `Hello` LF `World` is `Hello.  World`. A blank line writes `.` CR CR LF. A paragraph ends before its pieces measure more than 5,001 characters (section 3.3). A colon early in a line, or followed by more words, gets a space after it: `a:b` is `a: b`.
- Spaces become one, but two after `!`, `?` and a dot that ends a sentence, which depends on the word before the dot (an abbreviation, a number, a letter, a function word) and on whether the next word is capitalized: `Wow! nice. Yes` is `Wow!  nice.  Yes`.
- Some words are written out, alone or with a dot or a comma after them (section 3.5): `acct` is `account`, `Capt. Smith` `Captain Smith`, `gui` `gooey` and `january` `January`, and at a paragraph's end `aug` is `August.`.
- E-mail header lines are read too (sections 3.7 and 3.8). A `From:` line names its sender: `From: Jane Doe <jane@site.com>` is `From: Jane Doe.`, `From: jane@site.com (jane doe)` `From: Jane Doe.`. An `Author:` line names its authors: `Author: a@site.com (Jane Doe), bob@site.com` is `Author: Jane Doe, Bob.`. `To:`, `Cc:` and the lines of a header block that aren't headers are dropped, `Received:` starts a message (`Start of Message.`), `Subject:` and, in a header block, `Date:` are kept, and a `>` that starts a line goes.

**Exactness.** Checked against the original on 667,533 texts: each of the characters 1 to 255 but the line feed and the carriage return next to every rule's characters, dates and fractions with numbers of every length, signs, leading zeros and marks after them, words before and after the slash, dashes and brackets in every place, exponents, escape sequences, line ends, carriage returns, long lines with breaks at every place of their windows and runs of them, windows that end at the text's end, and 60,000 random texts of up to 4,577 characters. 659,470 gave the original's output. The other 8,063 are those section 4 lists. 28 recorded runs of the whole engine with texts in the spreadsheet context, tagged and untagged, at every mode, gave the original's audio and events ([engine.md](engine.md) §2).

Section 3 was checked against the original on 55,834 texts of up to 24,516 characters: lines of every length around the short line's, cut and whole, joined in every order; blank lines, carriage returns, tabs and runs of spaces and dots; paragraphs up to and past the limit, and last lines that do and don't fit; sentence ends after every kind of word, with every mark and quote after the dot, before capitalized and other words; paragraphs that start after sentence ends, blank lines and brackets; 10,000 random texts; each header's lines outside, in and after header blocks, quoted once and twice, in every order and with every message count; senders and authors with names, addresses, brackets and quotes; values and lines that end in commas, and held lines up to and past their limit; colons at every place of a line, with and without spaces and tabs around them; and 5,933 random e-mail texts. 51,847 gave the original's output. 445 are those section 4 lists, and the other 3,542 have words this section doesn't cover.

## 1. Lines

### 1.1 Windows

The pre-formatter reads its text, code page 1252 without the NUL, in **windows**. A window runs from where the reading stands to the next line feed, to the text's end, or for 720 characters, whichever comes first; it is **full** when it holds 720 characters and the text doesn't end there. The reading also knows whether it **continues** a line it cut, which it doesn't at the text's start.

1. A window that continues no line and isn't full is a line: its characters without their carriage returns. If the line is empty, it writes nothing, or CR LF if a line feed ends the window. Otherwise it writes the line (section 1.3), then CR LF if a line feed ends the window, else CR CR LF. The reading goes on after the line feed.
2. A full window that continues no line is cut at its **first cut** (section 1.2). The characters before it, without their carriage returns, are written as a line, then CR CR LF, and the reading goes on at the cut, continuing a line.
3. A window that continues a line is cut at its **cut**. With one, the characters before it, carriage returns kept, are written as a line, then CR CR LF, and the reading goes on at the cut, still continuing. Without one, the window is a line as in step 1, carriage returns kept, and the reading goes on continuing no line.

A carriage return is dropped from a window that continues no line even before a line feed: `a\r\nb` is `a` CR LF `b` CR CR LF.

### 1.2 Cuts

A **sentence break** is a dot followed by a space, even a space past the window's end.

A full window's **first cut** comes from a scan of its characters from the first. Each space is a space break, which it takes unless it takes the space right before it; each sentence break is one, which it takes unless it takes the sentence break two characters before it. In a run of spaces, it takes the first, the third and so on. The first cut is after the last sentence break it takes (after its space), else after the last space it takes, else at the window's end.

The **cut** of a window that continues a line is after its last sentence break, with the space after that break's space if there is one; else after its last space; else at its end if it is full. A window that isn't full and has neither has no cut.

### 1.3 Writing a line

A line is converted (section 2), then wrapped. The wrapping counts each character as a column, escape characters and tabs too, from the start of a **row**, the first at the line's start. At each character 77 columns or more past its row's start:
1. A space starts a new row after it.
2. A character that is the line's last, or before a space, is left alone.
3. Otherwise a new row starts after the row's last space but its first character. Without one, the character becomes a space, and a new row starts after it.

The rows aren't written apart: what shows of the wrapping is the characters it makes spaces.

## 2. A line

A line starts with its **escape sequences**: from its start, spaces and escape sequences, each `ESC [` up to the first letter after it (A to Z and a to z), the letter included. They are written as they are, to the last escape sequence's letter; the spaces after it go with the rest. An escape sequence without a letter takes the rest of the line. The rest of the line is converted in turn:
1. If it has two slashes or more, its date (section 2.1), and nothing else.
2. If it has one slash, its fraction (section 2.2).
3. If it then has a dash: if its first dash is a minus sign, its minus signs (section 2.3); else if its first dash is an exponent's, its exponent's dashes (section 2.5); else nothing more is done.
4. Its brackets (section 2.4), then its exponents (section 2.5).

A **number** is read in 16 bits as C's `atoi` reads one: spaces, tabs, line feeds, vertical tabs, form feeds and carriage returns, a `+` or a `-`, and the digits up to the first other character, modulo 65536. `65537` is 1, `-1` 65535, `x1` 0.

The words are those of the data pack's `preformatter.json` ([datapack.md](datapack.md) §15): `minus` is its `minus` word followed by a space.

### 2.1 Dates

The **month** is the characters from the last space before the first slash, or the line's start, up to the slash. If its number is 1 to 12 and it has three characters at most, the month and the slash become the month's name and a space, and every later slash a comma and a space: `12/25/1999` is `December 25, 1999`, `x 3/1/2` `x March 1, 2`, `03/1//` `March 1, , `. Otherwise the line is left as it is.

### 2.2 Fractions

The **numerator** is the characters before the slash back to the last space before them, the character right before the slash included even when it is a space; or nothing when the slash is the line's first character. If it has a dash, only what follows the first dash counts. The **token** is the characters after the slash up to the next space or the line's end. If the numerator's number or the token's is 0, the line is left as it is.

The token's **marks** are the `! , . : ; ?` at its end; the rest is the **denominator**. The fraction is written:
- for a denominator of three characters or more: `/`, the denominator and the marks in reverse order: `1/100.!` is `1 /100!.`;
- for one of one or two characters: its word, `s` unless the numerator's number is 1, and the marks in reverse order. The word of a denominator of one character, a digit 1 to 9, is its `denominators` word: 1 `one`, 2 `half`, 3 `third` and so on. One of two characters is read as tens and units:

  | Tens | Units | Word |
  |---|---|---|
  | `1` | a digit | its `teens` word: 10 `tenth` to 19 `nineteenth` |
  | `1` | another character | the first `otherUnits` word, `one` |
  | `2` to `9` | `0` | its `tens` word: 20 `twentieth` to 90 `ninetieth` (40 is `fourtieth`) |
  | another character | `1` to `9` | the tens, `0`, a space and its `ordinals` word: 23 `20 third`, 05 `00 fifth` |
  | another character | another character | the tens, `0`, a space and the second `otherUnits` word, `inetieth` |

The line becomes its characters up to the slash, a space, the fraction and, if characters follow the space after the token, a space and them. A space right after the token at the line's end is dropped: `1/2 ` is `1 half`.

If the line's first space after its leading spaces comes before the slash, and neither character next to it is a dash, `and` and a space are written after it: `3 1/2` is `3 and 1 half`, `1 /2` `1 and  half`. Then, for as long as the line holds ` and -` or `- and `, each ` and -` becomes ` -`, then each `- and ` `- `, those the text had included.

### 2.3 Minus signs

The line's first dash is a **minus sign** when only spaces come before it, or when it isn't the line's last character and the characters before and after it are both below `A` as signed bytes: below 65, or 128 and up. A line whose first dash is a minus sign is written again: its leading spaces as they are, then each dash as `minus`, after a space unless what is written ends in one, each space unless what is written ends in a space past the leading ones, and each other character as it is. `5 - 3` and `5-3` are `5 minus 3`, `x - y` is `x minus y`, and `x -y` and `a-b` stay as they are.

### 2.4 Brackets

In a line with a `(` and a `)`, the line is written again: its leading spaces as they are, then each `(` as `minus`, no `)`, each space unless what is written ends in a space past the leading ones, and each other character as it is. `(12)` is `minus 12`, `( 3 )` `minus 3 `.

### 2.5 Exponents

The line's first dash is an **exponent's** when it isn't a minus sign, follows an `E` after a digit or a dot, and isn't the line's last character. In such a line, each `E-` becomes ` times 10 to the minus `, then each `E+` ` times 10 to the `, then each `E minus ` ` times 10 to the minus `, before the brackets and the exponents: `2E-3` is `2 times 10 to the minus 3`.

The **exponents**: if the line's first `E` starts `E+` or `E minus ` (with the space), each `E+` becomes ` times 10 to the `, then each `E minus ` ` times 10 to the minus `. `1.5E+10` is `1.5 times 10 to the 10`, and `4E -2`, whose dash is a minus sign, `4 times 10 to the minus 2`. The words `times 10 to the` are the data pack's `exponent`.

## 3. The address and e-mail contexts

In the address and e-mail contexts, the pre-formatter reads the text in the windows of sections 1.1 and 1.2, but converts and wraps nothing: it reads e-mail messages' header lines, joins the text's lines into **paragraphs**, spaces each paragraph's words, writes out the words of a list and writes it, followed by CR CR LF. This section covers how it reads, joins and spaces a text, and the header lines. What it makes of some words isn't ported yet: it reads out some abbreviations by the words around them (`St.` is `Saint` or `Street`, `ala. is` `Alabama is`, `Mr Smith` `Mister Smith`), list words in brackets and quotes, and smileys (`;)` is `wink`), spells some words (`gre` is `g-r-e`, `BCC` `B-C-C`), reads some letters as words (`A` is `aye`), splits some marks from words (`hello#` is `hello #`), reads emphasis (`**bold**`), a dot inside a word (`cat.dog`), numbers, runs of single letters and the values of `Date:` lines its own way. This section doesn't cover texts with such words.

### 3.1 Pieces

Each window gives a **piece**, its characters without the line feed, carriage returns dropped as section 1.1 has it: a **cut piece** when the window is cut, a **line** when a line feed ends it, and the **last line** when it runs to the text's end. A piece is **cleaned**: its tabs become spaces, each run of two spaces or more becomes one space unless it follows a dot, and its leading spaces are dropped. Then its colons are spaced when the line it was read as gets them, but where section 3.7 says otherwise. Its **length** is the cleaned piece's, the colons' spaces counted.

A line's colons get spaces when, without its leading spaces and one `>` at its start, its first colon has no space or tab before it or right after it, and either a space or a tab comes after the colon or the colon is the line's 2nd to 12th character. Then each colon of the piece that no space or tab follows gets a space after it: `To:` is `To: `, `a:b:c` `a: b: c` and `see:the list` `see: the list`; `Dear Sir:`, TAB `To:`, `:ab`, `> a:b` and `abcdefghijkl:m` stay as they are.

### 3.2 Paragraphs

The pre-formatter starts with an empty paragraph and takes each piece but the last line in turn, those the e-mail reading leaves (section 3.7) and as it leaves them:

1. A cut piece of one character at most, leading spaces and tabs and trailing spaces aside, ends the paragraph. If the paragraph has anything, it is written (section 3.4), then the character as a paragraph of its own: its marks are read, and the next paragraph starts where it ends. Otherwise the character is written as it is, with CR CR LF, and the next paragraph starts where the one before it ended. A line of words over 720 characters long that ends in `apple. a you`, then `Garden`, gives `apple.` CR CR LF `a` CR CR LF `you. Garden`; with `_` for the `a` the middle paragraph is `~`, and with no character, `apple.    you`, it is a space and `you.  Garden` starts a sentence.
2. An empty line, or a line of spaces, ends the paragraph too. If the paragraph has anything, it is written, then `.` and CR CR LF, and the next paragraph starts a sentence (section 3.4); otherwise CR LF is written.
3. If the paragraph has anything and the piece doesn't fit in it (section 3.3), the paragraph is written.
4. The piece is added to the paragraph:
   - a piece that ends in a colon, or a short line, with `.  ` after it. A line is **short** when its length, with the characters the paragraph holds from the pieces cut from the same line before it, and 1 more if the paragraph holds a line before those, is under 39;
   - a line that ends in a dot and spaces, as it is;
   - any other piece without its trailing spaces, followed by a space.
5. If the piece didn't fit, the paragraph is written, the piece alone in it.

`Hello` LF `World` is `Hello.  World` CR CR LF, then ` ` CR CR LF for the text's end (section 3.6). `Dear Sir:` LF `ok` is `Dear Sir:.  ok`, `Note:` LF `ok` is `Note: . ok`, and `end.` LF `ok`, whose two dots section 3.4 makes one, `end.  ok`. `one` LF LF LF `two` is `one.` CR CR LF `.` CR CR LF CR LF `two` CR CR LF ` ` CR CR LF.

### 3.3 The paragraph limit

The paragraph's **size** is the sum of the lengths of the pieces added to it, a cut piece's without its trailing spaces and 1 more if it has any, with 2 more for each line. A piece **doesn't fit** when the size and twice its length, trailing spaces and all, with 2 more for a line, come to more than 5,001. The last line doesn't fit when the size and twice its length without its trailing spaces, plus 1, come to more than 5,001.

Near the limit, a paragraph is written differently. Its **raw size** is the sum of what its pieces count: a line its length and 2, and 1 more for a short line; a cut piece its length without its trailing spaces, and 1; a held line added to it (section 3.7) its length and 3. In a line or a cut piece, each run of dots and the spaces after it counts 1 less for each of its dots and spaces after the fifth, 2 less at most. The last line doesn't count. A paragraph whose raw size is 5,000 or more, or 5,001 or more when a separator (section 3.4, step 3) stands twice in a row in it, is **long**, and its pieces are joined this way instead of as section 3.2, step 4 has it:
- a piece that ends in a colon, and a short line, with `.  ` after it, as in any paragraph;
- a line that ends in spaces, with them, followed by a **kept gap** and a space; a line that ends in a comma or a dash, followed by a space, a kept gap and a space, but not one that ends in a comma after a space, a comma, a dash, a `+` or a slash;
- any other piece without its trailing spaces, followed by a space.

A kept gap and the space after it at the paragraph's end go. Of a long paragraph's marks only its `(` change, instead of as section 3.4 reads them: each `(` with no space before it goes, but at the paragraph's start and after a dot, a comma, a dash, a `+` or a slash. Faces, separators and the rest stay as they are. Then the spacing (section 3.4) reads a long paragraph so:
- Before step 1, the first dash of each run of two dashes or more after a character other than a space becomes a space: `a--b` is `a - b`.
- In step 3, a run of dots that spaces follow becomes a dot, ` .` for each of its dots and spaces after the third, 2 at most, and a space when the paragraph's part before it, from its last space or its last comma that follows a character other than a comma or a space, with a dot after it, is of kind word (no sentence starting); after any other part, a dot, ` .` if its dots and spaces are more than four, and a space. A run that this gives no ` .` is read as in any paragraph: `the end.   More` is `the end. .  More`, `the end...  More` `the end. . .  More`, and `the end.  More` stays as it is.
- A kept gap is a word of its own that steps 1 to 13 leave as it is, and then the spaces beside it go. The words before and after it are read as if two spaces stood between them, two spaces are written for it, and the word after it is read with no sentence starting. A line that isn't short and ends in `ran,` or `ran  `, then a line `away`, give `ran,  away` and `ran  away`.

When the paragraph is written, a gap of two spaces or more after some words is **widened**: it gets one or two spaces more when, with them, 5,000 characters or more of the written paragraph come before the word after it (in the paragraph's order, the gaps before it widened as they are). The spaces it may get are:
- after a word that ends in a dot (section 3.4, step 1), spaced as a word, a start or a function word, whose dot isn't closed: its **typed** spaces, with 1 more if its dotted part exclaims and 1 more if the word after it is capitalized and it isn't spaced as a function word, less 2. A word's typed spaces are those after it in the paragraph, 2 at most, or 1 before a kept gap; a start's and a function word's are 1;
- after a word that exclaims (section 3.4, step 2): one before a kept gap, or before a capitalized word that gets two spaces because a sentence starts and words were skipped.

`the end.  More` is `the end.   More` when 5,000 characters or more would come before `More`, and `the end!.  More` `the end!.    More`, while `the end!  More` and `the end.  more` stay as they are.

### 3.4 Writing a paragraph

A paragraph's marks are read first, but a long one's (section 3.3):
1. The **faces** are read: `:-)`, `8-)` and `:)` become `ha ha`, `;-)` and `;)` `wink`, `:-o` and `:o` `uh oh`, `:-(` `groan`, `:-/` `oh yeah?` and `|-O` `yawn`. Each face in this order is read from the paragraph's start to its end while there is **room** for it: the room starts as 4,999 less the paragraph's raw size (section 3.3), and each face read takes its reading's length and 2, less the face's length, from it; a face that would take all the room left, or more, stays as it is. The reading gets a space before it unless what is read of the paragraph so far ends in a space, and one after it unless a space follows the face: `so ;) ok` is `so wink ok`, and `so;)ok` `so wink ok`.
2. Then the **arrows** are read the same way, with the room the faces left: `<-->`, `<==>`, `<->` and `<=>` become `to and from`, and `-->`, `==>`, `->` and `=>` become `to`, in this order: `a->b` is `a to b`, and `x <=> y` `x to and from y`.
3. The **separators** are `%% && ** ++ -- // :: ;; << == @@ ^^ __ || ## >> +- +_ -= -_ <>`. In this order, each that stands twice in a row somewhere in the paragraph goes everywhere in it, and a run of it between two spaces goes with the space before it: `a----b` is `ab`, `a ---- b` `a b`, `a--b----c` `abc` and `a+-+-b` `ab`, while `a--b` keeps its dashes. Then, if two backslashes stand together, every backslash goes: `a \ b \\ c` is written `a b c`.
4. Each run of the same `!` or `?` becomes one. Then a word of `!` and `?` only, after a space and longer than four characters, keeps its first four: `Hi!!! no??` is `Hi! no?`, `what !?!?!? now` `what !?!? now`.
5. Each `"!"` and `(!)` is read as `"exclamation!"` and `(exclamation!)`, with a space before it unless a space comes before it, and one after it unless a space follows. Then each word `!s`, and then each word `!'s`, after a space and before one, becomes `, exclamations!,` with the space before it. Then `?` is read the same way, as `question`: `cat"!"dog` is `cat "exclamation!"  dog`, and `cat ?'s dog` `cat, questions?,  dog`.
6. A word `;` after a space becomes `semicolon`, and a word `:` after a space `colon`.
7. Each `~` after a space, or after the `approximately ` an earlier one became, becomes `approximately ` (with a space): `about ~5 and ~~` is `about approximately 5 and approximately approximately `.
8. The **brackets**. Their room is 4,999 less the paragraph's length, counted with the spaces that went with separators in step 3 and as if step 5 had put a space on each side of each quoted mark. Each `(` loses the spaces after it and then, if the paragraph has a `(`, each `)` the spaces before it, a dot taking the place of two spaces or more after a dot; each space that goes adds one to the room. Then each `(` in turn takes one from the room: while the room is above 0, it gets a space before it unless a space comes right before it; after that, one with no space before it goes, but at the paragraph's start and after a dot, a comma, a dash, a `+` or a slash. Then, if the paragraph had a `(`, each `)` in turn takes one from the room: while the room is above 0, it gets a space after it unless a space, a comma, a dot or `?` follows it or it ends the paragraph; after that, it loses the spaces after it when a comma, a dot or a `)` follows them. `go ( see )ok` is `go (see) ok`, `cat(dog) ok` `cat (dog) ok`, and `a.  )x (b` `a. .) x (b`.
9. Each `|` before a space or at the end becomes a comma: `cat|(dog` is `cat, (dog`.

If that leaves only spaces, the paragraph is written as a space and CR CR LF. Otherwise it is spaced (below), its **words of a `>`** are read and its words lose their **square brackets** (below), and it is written without its trailing spaces, followed by CR CR LF. A paragraph that starts with `(` keeps the space step 8 puts before it: `(see below)` is ` (see below)`.

The spacing first splits the paragraph's words at their changes of case (section 3.11), and then changes the paragraph. A **sign** is `+`, `-` or `/`. A part of a word beside a slash **keeps** the slash when, without a `>` at its start and up to the first comma that step 9 would space in it, it has a small letter (`a` to `z`), or is lowered (section 3.9), or has only the marks `! " ( ) , . : ; ? [ ]`, or nothing: `cat a/X,y dog` is `cat a X, y dog`, and `XB/dog` `x B / dog`, while `AB/dog` is `A-B dog`. What step 11 would make a `~` is read in small letters for this, the part after a word's last slash with what follows the word in the paragraph and any other as if a space followed it: `cat x/_A_ dog` is `cat x / ~a dog`, while `cat a/_A_.  Dog` is `cat a _A_.  Dog` and `cat a/_A_,b dog` `cat a _A_, b dog`.
1. In each word that starts with no digit or dot, a comma right before a `+` that follows no sign becomes two commas. Then, in each word, a comma that **gets a space** (step 9) gets it when, past the commas after it, a sign follows: `x,+y` is `x, , + y` and `a,/b` `a, / b`.
2. Two dots after a character that is no dot, followed in their word by a dash or a slash that no other dash or slash follows, lose that dash or slash, which becomes a space if more of the word follows, when:
   - it is a dash that step 7 would make a space or drop, taken with no next word;
   - it is a slash that no other slash follows in the word, the word's part before the dots, from its last comma, with a dot after it, is of a kind other than word (below, no sentence starting), and the part before the slash, or the part after it (or the next word that isn't empty, when nothing follows it), doesn't keep it.

   `go Mr..- Smith` is written `go Mr.  Smith`, and `1../2` `1. 2`.
3. Each run of dots, with the spaces after it, changes, in the paragraph as the runs before it left it. The run is **attached** when no space follows it and it isn't at the paragraph's end. It comes **after a single character** when the paragraph's part before it, from its last space or its last comma that follows a character other than a comma or a space, is one character other than a digit. What follows the run **breaks off** a single character when it starts with a run of `! ? ) ] : ; . , " '`, two characters or more, with a `)`, `]`, `"` or `'` after its first character, or with commas followed by a letter or a digit. With _d_ dots and _s_ spaces:
   - One attached dot after a single character that what follows breaks off becomes a dot and a space: `go x.))) Next` is `go x. ))) Next`, and `go a.,b ok` `go a. ,b ok`. One attached dot after a single `!` or `?` becomes `.  ` before a letter: `wow !.Then` is `wow !.  Then`. Any other dot followed by one space or none stays as it is.
   - Two attached dots are a **dot pair**, read with the paragraph's **part** before them, from its last space or comma, and the part's **closing marks**, the `! ? ) ] : ; .` at its end. What follows them **closes** when it starts with a run of `! ? ) ] : ; .`, a quote perhaps ending it, followed by a space, a comma or the paragraph's end; the run takes in that comma when no `! ? ) ] : ; . , "` follows it. The pair's **closing run** is the part's closing marks, the two dots and that run. The pair becomes:
     - `.  ` before a `+`: `cat..+dog` is `cat.  + dog`;
     - a dot when what follows closes with a run of three characters or more and the part has two characters or more before its closing marks: `go cat..)!) ok` is `go cat.)!)  ok`;
     - a dot and a **kept space** when the part, with a dot after it, is of kind word (below, no sentence starting), or is a function word with a closing run of more than four characters, or ends in a quote and has a core (below) of two characters or more, and what follows **splits** the word or is a slash that the part, with a dot after it, keeps. What follows splits it when it starts with a comma or a dash; with a quote before a space or the paragraph's end; with a run of `!` and `?` before a space, a comma, the paragraph's end, or a dot and one space that no other space follows; or with one of `) ] : ; ! ?` before a space, the paragraph's end, a comma, a slash or a dash, before another of those or a quote and then a space or the paragraph's end, or before a dot and one space that no other space follows. `cat.., dog` is `cat. , dog`, `cat..! dog` `cat. !  dog`, `the cat../dog` `the cat.  / dog` and `go cat"..,  ok` `go cat". , ok`. So does a slash after a part that, with a dot after it, is a list word other than a plain one (section 3.5): `my acct../ is here` is `my account. is here`;
     - `. .` after a single character that what follows breaks off, or that what follows doesn't start with a slash, a `(`, a dash or a **run of signs**: a run of `, ; : ! ?` before a space, a dot or the paragraph's end, or with a comma after its first character, in a first word (up to its first space) with no letter or digit. `a..b ok` is `a. .b ok`;
     - a **dropped dot** and a dot when the part is one letter and what follows starts with a run of signs, or the part is a letter, a digit or `_` followed by `) ] : ; ! ?` with a `!` or `?` among them: `go a!..b ok` is `go a!.b ok`, and `go a..,, ok` `go a., , ok`;
     - otherwise a dot: `cat..dog` is `cat.dog`, `the..! dog` `the.!  dog` and `1..b ok` `1.b ok`.
   - Any other run becomes `.  ` followed by `. ` once for each six of _d_ + _s_ − 2, _s_ counted as 1 if it is 0: `end...  More` is `end.  More`, `end.` and seven spaces before `More` `end.  .  More`. An attached run whose dots past those sixes are two at most writes the last of those `. ` as `.`, which stays on the word: `cat.......dog` is `cat.  .dog`.

   A kept space is written as a space but spaced as a character of the word: the word on both sides of it is one word. A dropped dot isn't written, and the spacing reads a word as if it weren't there, but where this section says otherwise. Then each number word's dot before two spaces or more gets a dot of its own (section 3.5).
4. Each `;` or `:` after a dot and spaces, before a space or the paragraph's end, becomes `semicolon` or `colon`: `end...; ok` is `end.  semicolon ok`.
5. While the paragraph starts with `. .  `, not followed by a run of `!` and `?` before a space or the paragraph's end, the `. .  ` becomes `. `.
6. A `+` right after the run of `!` and `?` that starts a word becomes a space, and so on in what follows it: `!+?+b` becomes `! ? b`. Each other `+` gets a space before it unless a space comes before it or it starts the paragraph, and one after it unless a space follows, it ends the paragraph, or a run of `!` and `?` that ends its word follows: `a+b` is `a + b`, and `a+! ok` is written `a +!  ok`.
7. In each word, each run of two dashes or more becomes ` - ` for each of its dashes, without the first space when the run starts a word with a space before it: `a--b` is `a - - b`, and `the cat. -- is here` `the cat. - - is here`, its dot followed by one space. A single dash, with the word's characters before it and after it:
   - at the word's start or after a slash, stays, with a space after it unless only `!` and `?` follow it: `-5` is `- 5`;
   - between two digits, stays: `1-2`;
   - after an opening `" ' ( [` with no dash between them, stays with a space after it when a closing `" ' ) ]` comes close after it: the last one in the characters after it, their commas spaced as step 9 has it and up to the first space that leaves, or in the next word for a dash that ends its word, is at most their third character, with no dash or slash before it. But not when those characters, or that next word, are three characters or more, end in a dot and are followed by two spaces or more, or by one at the paragraph's end. `go (a-b) ok` is `go (a- b) ok`, `go (a-bc) ok` `go (a- bc) ok`, `go (a- bcd) ok` `go (a bcd) ok`, and `go (a- b).  Then` `go (a b).  Then`;
   - otherwise, becomes a space, or goes at the word's end: `well-known` is `well known`.
8. Each word's slashes, the words taken from the paragraph's end, the **next word** being the first word after it that isn't empty, up to its first space, as this step left it, and the **word after it** the first word after it that isn't empty as it was before this step. A word that ends in a slash after something and the word after it are a **path** when that word has a slash after something. The slashes that start the word stay, each with a space after it, and the rest of the word, its **body**, is read:
   - A body that starts with `(` or `>` and a slash: if another slash follows, the `(` or `>` and the slash go and what follows is read as a body. A `(` and a slash that end the word go, and nothing of the body stays, when the word after it starts with a slash. Otherwise, if the part after the slash, up to the next one, doesn't keep it, and for a `>` doesn't start with a digit, the `(` or `>` and the slash go, an **opening gone** (section 3.10), and every other slash stays with a space after it. When nothing follows the slash, that part is, for a `(`, the word after it up to its first slash, and for a `>` the next word.
   - In a body with something between two of its slashes, or with two slashes or more in a word of a path, every slash stays with a space after it, but the first of a run of slashes after something, when a letter or a digit comes later in the body: `go a/b///c ok` is `go a / b / / c ok`.
   - In a body with two slashes or more and nothing between them, the first slash becomes a space, and every other a `/` of its own, with a space before it.
   - A single slash stays with a space after it in a word of a path, or when the part before it and the part after it, or the next word when nothing follows it, both keep it, with a space before it too when the part before it is a run of `!` and `?`. Otherwise, and when nothing follows it at the paragraph's end, it becomes a space.

   A slash that becomes a space takes the kept spaces at the end of the part before it with it, unless that part is a list word other than a plain one (section 3.5). A list word (section 3.5), or a word the letter rules write (sections 3.9, 3.11 and 3.12), before a slash that stays drops it, but not in a path: `DOg/x` is `DO-g x` and `bCD/x` `b-C-D x`, while `cat acct/(/A dog` is `cat account / A dog`. A word that ends in a slash after something loses the space after it, and a word that leaves nothing goes, with one of the spaces beside it: `a/b` is `a / b`, `/usr/bin` `/ usr / bin`, `a//b` `a / b`, `A/B` `A B`, `the end/` `the end`, `go (/a) ok` `go (/ a) ok`, `go (/A) ok` `go A) ok` and `my gui../ 5 is here` `my gooey. 5 is here`. `go A/ B/C ok` is `go A / B / C ok`, while `go A/ B ok` is `go A B ok`, and `go (/ /a ok` is `go / a ok`. Then the dots of some list words get a space after them (section 3.5).
9. A comma with a character before it **gets a space** if that character is no digit, dot, comma or sign, or is a dot that follows a character other than a dot, and the word goes on after the comma; if that character is a digit, and the commas after it are followed by a character other than a digit, a dot, a comma or `(`, and other than a dash or a slash in a word that starts with a digit or a dot; or if that character is a dot that starts the word, and the commas and dots after it are followed by such a character. In each word that doesn't start with a comma, a space comes after the first comma that gets one, and what follows it is read as a word of its own: `a,b,c` is `a, b, c`, `ab.,y` `ab., y`, `5,a` `5, a`, `5,,a` `5, ,a`, `Hi,. there` `Hi, . there` and `.,.x` `., .x`; `5,6` and `.,.5` stay as they are. When the first word this leaves is lowered (section 3.9), it is the whole word, the words after it following: `XB,.` is `x B,. .`, `XB,!` `x B,!!` and `XB,dog` `x B,dog dog`.
10. A run of five `!` and `?` or more after a space, a `+` or a `-`, before a space or the paragraph's end, keeps its first four: `cat -!?!?! dog` is `cat -!?!?  dog`.
11. A word that starts with `_` becomes `~` when, without a comma, a slash, or a dot that exactly one space follows at its end, it is only `_` or ends in `_`. The `~` is followed by the characters between its first and last runs of `_` without their `_`, in small letters (`A` to `Z` only), then that comma, slash or dot: `the _ cat` is `the ~ cat`, `the __x__, cat` `the ~x, cat`, `the _a_b_ cat` `the ~ab cat` and `cat _Dog_/x dog` `cat ~dog / x dog`, while `the __x__.  cat` keeps its underscores. The words of step 9 are words here, but one that ends in a comma that step 9 spaced keeps its underscores when the word it came from ends in neither a comma nor a dot: `cat _,b dog` is `cat _, b dog` and `cat x,_b_,y dog` `cat x, _b_, y dog`, while `cat _,b. dog` is `cat ~, b. dog` and `cat x,_b_ dog` `cat x, ~b dog`.
12. A word that starts with `*` and has a letter **opens** stars, unless a dot follows its only `*`: its `*` at its start become `~`, or `~*` when they are three and what follows them, without the slashes at its end, is one character. From that word on, the first word with a `*` past the `*` at its start closes them: that first run of `*` goes, with every `*` after it in the word, when no letter or digit follows it in the word, and stays otherwise. `cat *big* dog` is `cat ~big dog`, `cat *big red* dog` `cat ~big red dog`, `cat *big*s dog` `cat ~big*s dog`, `cat ***a dog` `cat ~*a dog` and `cat,*dog` `cat, ~dog`, while `cat *.b dog* ok` stays as it is.
13. A **closing run** is a run of the **closing marks** `! ? ) ] : ; . ,` and kept spaces, a quote perhaps ending it, before a space or the paragraph's end. It follows a character that is no closing mark, space or dropped dot, and that character follows one other than a space or a kept space; a dropped dot ends it. A closing run of more than four characters keeps only its last four when its word has two characters or more before it, a `>` at the word's start, the `(` after that and a `[` after them aside, and the others are **hidden**: they aren't written, but where this section says so a word is read with them. A kept space among the four that stay becomes a **cut space**: `go the.)))) Two` is `go the))))  Two`, and `go cat!..)) ok` `go cat. )) ok`. `go (ab!?!?!) ok` is `go (ab!?!)  ok` and `go [[a!?!?!) ok` `go [a!?!)  ok`, while `go (a!?!?!) ok` keeps its run.

Then it writes the spaces before the first word as they are, and each word after one space, or two where the rules below say. A word's cut spaces split it into **pieces**: the word is spaced as its first piece, read with the pieces after it where this section says so, and each other piece comes after one space, written as it is. Letters are `A` to `Z` and `a` to `z`, capitals `A` to `Z`, digits `0` to `9`. The spacing keeps track of whether a sentence **starts**, and counts the words it **skips** while one does.

A paragraph starts a sentence when the paragraph written before it ended where one starts, or ended at a blank line (section 3.2, step 2), or when one of its words **restarts** it: a word that starts with two digits or more, not all zeros, a dot and `)`, `]` or a `"` not followed by `)`, such as `12.)`. A paragraph that starts with `(` doesn't. The text's first paragraph starts one only when a word restarts it.

A word, read with its pieces, **ends in a dot** when its last character is a dot; when it ends in `."`; when it is a dot followed by one comma, or by `) ] : ;` that end it or that a quote ends; or when, without a quote at its end, it ends in a run of `) ] : ; , .`, two characters or more, that starts with a dot after a character other than `!` and `?`. A word with hidden marks ends in a dot only if it does when read with them too, and then as it does without them, or, if it doesn't without them, as it does with them. Its **dotted part** runs to that dot (in the last case, the one that starts the run), and a `)`, `]` or `"` right after the dot **closes** it. Its **core** is the dotted part without its dot, then without a `>` at its start that no digit follows, then without the `) ] ! ? : ; , .` at its end and the `( [` at its start. Its **kind** comes from its dotted part:
1. A dotted part ending in `".` is, by what comes before the quote without the `( [` at its start: a **word** if, without the `. ! ? ) ] : ;` at its end too, it is two characters or more; **empty** if it had such marks at its end; a **letter** if it is one letter, a **number** if it is one digit, and **empty** if it is one other character or nothing: `A". Bob` is `A".  Bob`.
2. Otherwise, by its core: a lowered dotted part (section 3.9) is a letter, as its small first letter is (`XRAY.. dog` is `x RAY. dog` and `XB.. BCD` `x B. B-C-D`); a core that ends in a dropped dot is empty; a core ending in `"`, longer than that quote, is a word; an empty core, or `"`, is **empty**; one character other than a letter or a digit is an initial, and more such characters a word; a digit followed by digits and dots is a **number**, or a word when a sentence starts and the dot is closed; one capital is an initial, one other letter a letter; a function word is a **start** when a sentence starts and a **function word** otherwise, but a word when a `[` is among the `> ( [` at the start of the word that ends in the dot; anything else is a word. `my [the. Is here` is `my the.  Is here`, while `my (the. Is here` and `my the. Is here` stay as they are.

The **function words** are the data pack's `address` `functionWords` ([datapack.md](datapack.md) §15), `about`, `after`, `and` and so on, case aside, and the **abbreviations** its `abbreviations`, `adj`, `asst` and so on, and the titles among its `words` (section 3.5): `my CAPT. Is here` stays as it is, while `my DOG. Is here` is `my DOG.  Is here`. A word ending in a dot is an abbreviation when its core, without a quote before the dot, is one, case aside. A word is **capitalized** when, without the `( " >` at its start, it starts with a capital that is alone or followed by a character other than a capital. A word is **skippable** when, without the `(` at its start, the `, ) ; : "` at its end and then a `~` at its start, it is a function word, or, without a `"` and then a dot at its start, a digit followed by digits, dots and dashes.

A word that ends in a slash, other than `/`, `>/` and one that ends in `(/`, has its slash **held**: the word loses it, and then the kept spaces at its end, and ` /`, or `  /` when a kept space came right before the slash, is written before the spaces before the next word, or at the paragraph's end. When the next word is a word of `!` and `?` only, ` /` is written after it instead, before the spaces before the word after it or at the paragraph's end: `the end/ ok` is `the end / ok`, `end../ Then` `end.  /  Then`, `my dog/ ! !` `my dog! /  !` and `my dog/ !` `my dog! /`.

A word **exclaims** when, read with its pieces, without a quote at its end and then the `, ) ] : ; .` and dropped dots at its end, it ends in `!` or `?`, and fewer than four characters were taken off it. Each word in turn:
1. A word that ends in a dot. If the closing marks and quotes at its end, its hidden ones and those of its pieces too, and one for each cut space, are more than four, a function word or a start is spaced as a word, and an initial as a letter.
   - The spaces after it are two if it exclaims, but with fewer than three characters taken off it when two spaces or more follow it and it is no word, start or function word. Otherwise, with two spaces or more after it in the paragraph, two after a word, one after a number or a function word, and after the other kinds two before a capitalized word, else one. With one space after it, one after an abbreviation that is neither a start nor one of the data pack's `spacedAbbreviations`, `lat` and `long`; otherwise two after a word, a letter, a start or an empty one before a capitalized word, else one. A dotted part that is one character or a number, closing marks with a `!` or `?` among them and its dot, with no quote before the dot, is spaced as an empty one: `go 12!)].  Next` stays as it is, and `go 12!)].  next` is `go 12!)]. next`. The next word is read lowered (section 3.9) for whether it is capitalized: `my dog. Xbox is here` is `my dog. x box is here`.
   - A word of kind word, start or function word with two spaces or more after it, or a held slash with a kept space before it, whose last character is its dot, keeps only the last three characters of the run of `) ] : ; ! ? .` it ends in, when that run is longer than three and something comes before it: `end;!).  Next` is `end!).  Next`, and `go cat.)).  Next` `go cat)).  Next`. Then, if it ends in `".`, it loses its quote and the `. ! ? ) ] : ;` before the quote, unless a comma comes before those or the next word is a word of `!` and `?` only: `"Stop!".  Go` is `"Stop.  Go`.
   - If a sentence starts and the word is a number, it is skipped. Otherwise a sentence starts after it when it is a start, or neither a function word, a number nor an abbreviation, and the count of skipped words starts again.
2. A word that exclaims has two spaces after it: `go cat!.) Next` is `go cat!.)  Next`. Without its quote and marks at its end, then the `!` and `?` at its end and then the kept spaces at its end, if it ends in a dot that doesn't follow one of `) ] : ; , ! ?`, a sentence starts after it as step 1 has it; if not, it is skipped when a sentence starts and it is skippable; otherwise no sentence starts after it, and the count starts again.
3. A word whose part before its first kept space ends in a dot: if that part's dot follows one of `) ] : ; ! ?` and the part after the kept space starts with one of `) ] : ; ,`, the word has two spaces after it when its dotted part exclaims, no sentence starts after it, and the count starts again: `go cat!.., ok` is `go cat!. ,  ok`. Otherwise it has the spaces step 1 gives its dotted part before one space, a sentence starts after it as step 1 has it for that part, and the count of skipped words is 1 if one starts and the part after that kept space, up to the next one, is only `, ; )`, and 0 otherwise.
4. Any other word is skipped when a sentence starts and it is skippable; otherwise no sentence starts after it, and the count starts again.

A list word is then written out (section 3.5), and so is a word that the letter rules change (section 3.9). Before each word but the first come two spaces if a sentence starts, words were skipped and the word, read lowered (section 3.9), is capitalized; otherwise the spaces the word before it gave, or one. A word of `!` and `?` only comes right after the word before it, unless that word is one too and isn't the first: with no space, or with one when the word before it ends in a dot, has two spaces or more after it in the paragraph, and is a word or lost characters to the cut in step 1, or when it held a slash that came right after a kept space: `my dog../ ! is here` is `my dog. ! /  is here`. When it comes with no space so after a word that ends in a quote, that word is written with its hidden marks, and then the closing marks and quote at its end again (their last four) when they are more than four, or when something that is no number (a dot may start it) comes before them, the `(` at the word's start and a `[` after those aside; with only those before them, when a `[` is among them and the word so written loses it (below). `cat a!" ! dog` is `cat a!"!"!  dog`, `cat 1!" ! dog` `cat 1!"!  dog` and `cat [!" ! dog` `cat !"!"!  dog`. The paragraph **ends where a sentence starts** when one starts after its last word. `Wow! nice. Yes` is `Wow!  nice.  Yes`, `See p. Two` is `See p.  Two`, `Item 3. Next` stays as it is, and `Done.` LF `by the Way` is `Done.  by the  Way`, while `my dog. The Xbox is here` is `my dog.  The x box is here`. `a ! ? b` is `a!  ?  b`, `hi.  ? ok` `hi. ?  ok` and `the)).  ? no` `the)).?  no`.

The words of a `>`, after the spacing: a run of words `>`, the first at the paragraph's start or after a space and each other after one space, with the spaces before and after the run. If spaces and a digit follow it, the run becomes one `>` after the spaces before it, with a space more if the run has two `>` or more, and the spaces after it. Otherwise, if spaces come before it, and something comes before them and after the spaces after it, it becomes two spaces. Otherwise only its spaces stay. Then each `>` that starts a word and is followed by a character other than a space or a digit goes: `x > > 5 dogs` is `x  > 5 dogs`, `x > 5` stays as it is, `a > hi` is `a  hi` and `a >b >5` `a b >5`.

Then the **square brackets**: a word that starts with `[`, after the `(` at its start, loses the `[` when what follows it, up to a word of `!` and `?` that came right after it with no space, is, without a quote at its end and then the `] ) ! ? . , ; : / -` before that, two characters or more and, without a quote at its end and then a dot at its start, no number: `go [ab] ok` is `go ab] ok`, `go [cat- ok` `go cat ok`, `cat [a!") dog` `cat a!") dog` and `cat [1!") dog` `cat 1!") dog`, while `go [a] ok`, `go [12] ok`, `go [12.5] ok`, `cat [a!" dog` and `cat [a)" dog` stay as they are. Two words have this settled by the spacing, after each word, instead: one with hidden marks loses its `[`, and one with two spaces or more after it in the paragraph whose last character is a dot is read without that dot. Either keeps a `[` that comes right after the one it loses, and a `>` after it doesn't start the word for the words of a `>`: `cat [a". dog` is `cat a". dog` while `cat [a"` LF is `cat [a".`, and `go [>a!?!?!" ok` is `go >a!?!"  ok`.

### 3.5 List words

The data pack's `address` `words` ([datapack.md](datapack.md) §15) are the **list words**, each with its **expansion** and its **kind**, abbreviation, title, plain or capital: `acct` is `account`, `Capt` `Captain`, `gui` `gooey`, `pm` `p.m.`, `january` `January` and `ORG` `~ORG`, all abbreviations but `Capt`, a title, `gui`, plain, and `ORG`, a capital word. A word that section 3.4 spaces, without a slash it holds and the kept spaces before that slash, has **opening marks**, the characters before its first letter or digit, and **closing marks**, those after its last. It is a list word when what lies between them is one, case and all, or is read as one (section 3.12), and:
- an abbreviation or a title has opening marks of `(`, with a `>` perhaps at their start and one `[` perhaps among them, and closing marks of `) ] ! ? : ; , .`, with one kept space at most among them and a quote perhaps at their end, but no quote there when the next word is a word of `!` and `?` only;
- a plain or a capital word, and `NY` read as a plain one, may also have `"`, `'` and `~` among the `(` after the `[`, and closing marks that start with `"`, `'` or `*` and then have any of those marks, `"`, `'` and `*`.

`my (acct.) is` is `my (account) is`, `my "gui" is` `my "gooey" is` and `my NY* is` `my New York* is`, while `my "acct" is` and `my acct" ! is here` keep `acct`. An abbreviation of two capitals other than `NY` whose opening marks end in a `[` is one only when its closing marks are one dot, one space follows them and the next word, without the slash it holds, lowered (section 3.9) and without the `( [ "` at its start, doesn't start with a capital, isn't a number (a dot perhaps, a digit, then digits, dots, commas and dashes, then `) ] " ! ? : ; , .` perhaps) and isn't only `) ] ! ? : ; , . >`, a next word being needed. `my [AZ. is here` is `my Arizona is here`, while `my [AZ. Is here` is `my A-Z. Is here`, `my [AZ.  is here` `my A-Z.  is here` and `my [AZ. 5 is here` `my A-Z. 5 is here`. A **dotted** list word, `Mr`, `Mrs`, `Mt` and `Cntl` in small letters and capitalized, is one only when its closing marks are one dot, at most one space follows them, the next word isn't a word of `!` and `?` only and its slash, if it holds one, doesn't come after a kept space: `the Mr. ! ok` is `the Mr.!  ok`, and `my Mr.) Smith is` stays as it is.

A word that is no list word is one with an **ending** when what lies between its marks is a plain or capital word, or `NY`, followed by an apostrophe and what comes after it, or, when it is in capitals, followed by an `s`. The ending is the apostrophe and what follows it, or `'s` and then that. Its marks are a plain word's, and it is written after the expansion: `my gui's is here` is `my gooey's is here`, `my NYs is here` `my New York's is here` and `my ORGs is here` `my ~ORG's is here`.

A word or a piece of section 3.4 is **in capitals** when, lowered (section 3.9), it has no small letter and is none of `>` and `[`, a word of `! " ( ) , . : ; ? ]` only, and a number between marks: `"`, `(` and `[` perhaps, a dot perhaps, a digit, then digits, dots, commas and dashes, then `) ] ! ? : ; , . "` perhaps; nor `A` or `I` with a `>` and `(`s perhaps before it, unless a word of `!` and `?` only follows it or it is a letter of a run of letters (section 3.10). A word in capitals stands **near** a word when it is one of the two words before it or the two after it, pieces counting as words, each letter of a run of letters too (section 3.10), and a space at the paragraph's start as a word before its first. `THE (I` is `~THE (I`, while `THE A ! dog` is `THE A!  dog`.

- Plain or capital: one space, or two when its closing marks end in a dot and two spaces or more follow it: `gui. ok` is `gooey. ok`, `gui.  ok` `gooey.  ok` and `gui` `gooey`.

The next word doesn't count when other marks come before the dot and after it, unless a quote follows the dot. ### 3.6 The text's end

After the last piece:
- When the last line starts with Article (section 3.7), it is added to the paragraph, unquoted and without its leading and trailing spaces. The paragraph is written, then CR CR LF: `Article 5` is `Article 5` CR CR LF CR CR LF.
- Without a last line, when the text is empty or ends in a line feed, if the paragraph has anything, it is written, then a space and CR CR LF, without the space when the reading is in a header block (section 3.7); otherwise, or when a line that starts with Article is held, nothing is: `hi` LF is `hi.` CR CR LF ` ` CR CR LF, `hi` LF LF `hi.` CR CR LF `.` CR CR LF, `Article 5` LF `Article 6` LF `Article 5. Article 6.` CR CR LF CR CR LF, and `Article 5` LF `Article 6,` LF nothing.
- With a last line of one character, leading spaces and tabs and trailing spaces aside, if the paragraph has anything, it is written, then the character as a paragraph of its own; otherwise the character and CR CR LF are written: `x` is `x` CR CR LF, `_` `_` CR CR LF, and `hi` LF `_` `hi.` CR CR LF `~` CR CR LF.
- With a last line that has other characters and doesn't fit (section 3.3), while the paragraph has anything, the paragraph is written, then the last line as a paragraph of its own, and nothing more.
- Otherwise the last line, without its trailing spaces, is added to the paragraph. If the paragraph has anything, it is written, then a space and CR CR LF; otherwise CR CR LF is written.

### 3.7 E-mail messages

Before section 3.2 takes a piece, a cut piece too, the pre-formatter reads it as a line of an e-mail message. The piece's **line** is the piece without its leading spaces. A line is a **header line** when, after a `>` at its start if it has one, it starts with a header's name, as the data pack's `address` `headers` ([datapack.md](datapack.md) §15) write it, case and all, and a colon: `From: Jane` and `>From: Jane` are header lines, `from: Jane`, `From : Jane` and `> From: Jane` aren't. In and after a header block, a header marked `quotedTwice` may follow two `>`: `>>From: Jane` is a header line there. Its **value** is what follows the colon. A line is **blank** when it is empty or is `>`. A line **unquoted** is the line without its `>`, if it starts with one, leading spaces aside, and a space after that `>`: `> Hi` and `>Hi` are `Hi`.

Without its leading spaces and a `>` at its start, a piece is **colon-led** when its first colon is its 3rd character or later, and **tightly** colon-led when no space or tab comes before that colon either. A line **looks like a header** when, unquoted, cleaned (section 3.1), as the last line without its trailing spaces too, and its colons spaced if the line's get spaces, it has a colon followed by a space: `ab:cd` and `:ab c` look like headers, `:ab`, `a :b` and TAB `a:b` don't.

The reading is **outside** a header block at first. Header lines take it **in** a block, and a blank line in a block takes it **after** the block. It counts the **messages** that have started, and keeps whether the block has an Author line, a **held** line, and a value that **goes on**: an author's, a names value or a dropped one.

A line **starts with Article** when, without its leading spaces and a `>` at its start, it starts with `Article` followed by a space, a tab or nothing. The text's last line is read for this without its trailing spaces, its colons spaced if the line's get spaces, and then without its last character. `Article 5` and `>Article` start with Article, `Articles 5` and `Article: 5` don't. Such a line **starts a message**: outside a block the count of messages goes up by one, the reading is in a block, and no line goes on after a Subject line. The line is held if its last character is a comma and it isn't colon-led, and it goes to section 3.2 unquoted, so that the lines of a block after it write that paragraph first. Outside a block, only a line right after a line that is only spaces once unquoted and cleaned (section 3.1), or the text's first piece, starts a message so, and only until one has.

Each header has an action outside a block, one in a block and one after a block, or none there (the header's `outside`, `block` and `after`). A header with message counts (`outsideNames`, `insideNames`) is read as **names** instead once that many messages have started: outside a block the first count, in and after a block the second. The data pack's headers:

Each piece in turn:
1. If a line is held, it is let go. If the piece isn't colon-led and doesn't start with Article, the held line is **released**, and nothing more is done with the piece; otherwise the held line isn't written: `From: x` LF `foo,` LF `Article 7` LF is `From: X.` CR CR LF CR CR LF `Article 7.` CR CR LF CR CR LF, and `Article 5,` LF `Article 7` LF `Article 5,. Article 7.` CR CR LF CR CR LF. The reading is outside a block, and an empty line is added to the paragraph as section 3.2 adds a line, without step 2: it gets `.  `. Then, if the held line's and the piece's **measures** come to under 743, a cut piece's without its trailing spaces and a line's with 1 more, the held line, unquoted and cleaned, and `.  ` are added to the paragraph, and the next line's length counts from there (section 3.2, step 4). Then the piece, unquoted, goes to section 3.2 without step 2, its colons spaced if the piece's get spaces; as the last line, it doesn't count as one character (section 3.6). A line's measure is its length without its leading spaces and a `>` at its start, its tabs made spaces, each run of two spaces or more one unless it follows a dot, and its colons' spaces counted.
2. If a value goes on, it stops. Unless the piece is tightly colon-led, or the value is a dropped one and the line is blank, the piece goes on with it, and nothing more is done with the piece. If the piece, its trailing spaces and tabs aside, ends in a comma, the value goes on again. A dropped value's piece writes CR CR LF. 3. Outside a block, a line that starts with Article starts a message, as above. A header line is written (section 3.8). Any other line goes to section 3.2 unquoted.
4. In a block, a line that starts with Article starts a message. Otherwise:
   1. A line right after a Subject line **goes on** after it. If it is blank, CR LF is written for a line and CR CR LF otherwise, and the reading is after the block. Otherwise it is read as the next steps have it.

At a space, tab or colon, they are the word, the colon if it is one, then a space and the names (section 3.8) in the rest of the piece if those have anything. At a comma or semicolon, they are the word and the names in the rest of the piece from that character on. ### 3.8 Header lines

A header line's value first has its colons spaced if the header's name, a colon and the value, read as a line, get spaces (section 3.1). The value is **spaced** when it starts with a space or a tab, or its colons got spaces. If the value, its trailing spaces and tabs aside, ends in a comma, an author's or names value goes on into the next line (section 3.7), and so does a value of a header marked `continues`, as a dropped one.

Then the header line ends the paragraph: if it has anything, it is written (section 3.4). Then, by the action:
- **Dropped** and **block**: CR CR LF is written.
- **Message**: CR CR LF, the data pack's `messageStart`, `. ` and CR CR LF are written: `Received: x` LF is CR CR LF `Start of Message. ` CR CR LF.
- **Author**: the value's tabs become spaces, and each dot followed by two spaces or more becomes as many dots as there are spaces, then a space: `J.  Doe` is `J.. Doe`. The header's name and `:` are written, then a space and the value's names (below) if it has any, and `.` unless that ends in a dot. CR CR LF comes before them unless the line before was a Subject line, and after them CR LF if the piece is a line and the last name wasn't taken out of an address, else CR CR LF. `Author: jane doe` LF is CR CR LF `Author: Jane Doe.` CR LF.
- **Sender**, **names**, **kept**, **date** and **subject**: what the value gives is, for sender, the value's person, for names its names, and for the others the value cleaned (section 3.1) without its trailing spaces. The paragraph is the header's name and `:`, then a space if what the value gives has anything and the value is spaced, then what it gives. The header line is **short** when the piece is a line, the paragraph is under 38 characters, and it is no date and no person or names taken out of an address. A short header line gets `.  `, or `  ` if it ends in a dot; any other gets `.` unless it ends in one. The paragraph is written. `From: jane@site.com` LF is `From: Jane.` CR CR LF, and in a block `Organization:a b` LF is `Organization: a b.` CR CR LF and `Organization:ab` LF `Organization:ab.` CR CR LF.
- A subject line in or after a block that has an Author line always gets its space. As a line, its paragraph is instead the header's name, `: ` and the value cleaned, trailing spaces and all, ended this way: under 39 characters or ending in a colon, it gets `.  `, or `  ` if it ends in a dot; otherwise one that ends in a dot and spaces stays as it is, and any other loses its trailing spaces and gets a space; then it gets `.`. The paragraph is written.

Then the reading moves on:
- An author line, unless the line before was a Subject line, starts a message: the count goes up by one, and the block has an Author line. Outside a block, a block, message or sender line starts a message too, and if its header is marked `clearsAuthor`, the block has no Author line.
- After a subject line in or after a block, the reading is in the block and the next line goes on after it (section 3.7), unless the block has an Author line: then the reading is outside a block.
- After any other header line, the reading is in a block if it was in or after one, or if the line was a block, message or sender line, or an author line but right after a Subject line. Otherwise it stays outside.

A line that **goes on** after a Subject line, unquoted: if it is the text's last line, its leading spaces go, then its tabs become spaces and each run of two spaces or more one, unless it follows a dot. If the piece, its tabs made spaces, without its leading spaces, then a `>` at its start and then its trailing spaces, has one character at most, what is left of the line is written without leading and trailing spaces, or as it is if that leaves nothing, then CR CR LF: `>_` is `_` CR CR LF, and TAB ` ` CR CR LF. Otherwise the paragraph is what is left without leading and trailing spaces, and is written. A piece that isn't the last line is added as section 3.2 has it, but without step 2 and with its colons as they are, and the paragraph, if it has anything, is written.

The **person** in a value: the value's tabs become spaces, and its leading and trailing spaces go. A `(` or `<` is **free** when it comes at the value's start or after a space, comma or semicolon. The **delimiters** are `@ ! % / :` and an `at` after a space, comma or semicolon and before one or the value's end.
1. If the first free `(` follows a space and the word before it has an `@`, the person is what lies between it and the first `)` at the value's end or before a space, comma or semicolon, without leading and trailing spaces, if that has anything: `jane@site.com (Jane Doe)` is `Jane Doe`.
2. Otherwise, with a free `(`, the person is the part before it, up to its first delimiter. With no free `(` but a free `<`, it is the part before the `<` without leading and trailing spaces, and without a `"` at its start and one at its end: `"Jane Doe" <jane@site.com>` is `Jane Doe`. With neither, it is the value up to its first delimiter: `Jane at Site` is `Jane`.
3. The person is **taken out of an address** but in the last case, where it is only when the delimiter cut something off.
4. The person loses its leading and trailing spaces, each run of two spaces or more becomes one unless it follows a dot, and each word's first letter becomes a capital. A word starts at the person's start, after a space, comma or semicolon, and after a dash that follows another character. Each other capital becomes small unless the character before it is a small letter, a word's first letter counting as made a capital: `JANE mcDONALD` is `Jane McDonald`, and `jOHN dE` `John De`.

The **names** in a value: the value's tabs become spaces, and it is split at its commas and semicolons into **items**. Each item gives a name, with the item's leading spaces before it; the names, with the commas and semicolons between them, are made as a person is in step 4. They are taken out of an address when the last name is. An item's name:
1. If the item, without its leading and trailing spaces, has a `(` after a space, and the word before the first such `(` has an `@`, the name is what lies between that `(` and the first `)` at the item's end or before a space, without leading and trailing spaces, if that has anything.
2. Otherwise, with such a `(`, the name is the item before it, up to its first delimiter.
3. With no such `(` but a free `<`, the name is the item's part before the `<`, its leading and trailing spaces kept and what lies between them without a `"` at its start and one at its end.
4. With neither, the name is the item up to its first delimiter, followed by a space if one comes after the delimiter: `ab:cd ef, gh` is `Ab , Gh`, and `ab:cd, ef` `Ab, Ef`.
5. The name is taken out of an address but in the last case, where it is only when the delimiter cut something off. `Author: a@site.com (Jane Doe), bob@site.com` LF is CR CR LF `Author: Jane Doe, Bob.` CR CR LF.

### 3.9 Words in capitals

The **letter rules** read a word of section 3.4, read without its pieces, whose part between its opening and closing marks (section 3.5) is two capitals or more, starts with five capitals or more, is two capitals or more with signs and letters after them (below), or has a small head (section 3.12). The vowels are `A`, `E`, `I`, `O` and `U`; `Y` is neither a vowel nor a consonant, and the other letters are consonants, small letters counting as their capitals. Characters from 128 up, such as letters with accents, are no letters. The tables are the data pack's `address` `letters` ([datapack.md](datapack.md) §15).

When a word's opening marks end in two `[` or more, after a `>` and the `(` that may start them, the `[` after the first belong to its capitals, as letters that are neither vowels, `Y` nor consonants and are spelled as they are; the first goes or stays as the square brackets of section 3.4 say, and the word is written after it. `my [[AZ is here` is `my [-A-Z is here`, `my [[ORG is here` `my [-OR-G is here`, `my [[NNE is here` `my [-N-N-E is here` and `[[CYA` `[-CYA`, while `my [[NY is here` is `my [NY is here`.

A word's **signs** are the marks after its capitals before the longest closing marks that start with a quote, a `'` or a `*`, or that are `) ] ! ? : ; , .` and kept spaces with a quote perhaps at their end. A digit ends the capitals and their signs: it and what follows are written after the word as they are, and a quote, `'` or `*` before the digit goes with it. Signs belong to the capitals, as letters that are neither vowels, `Y` nor consonants and are spelled as they are, the word's length counting them, and the word is written before the rest of its closing marks: `AB~` is `A-B-~`, `AB~~` `A-B-~-~`, `IBM~` `eye-B-M-~`, `QUB~` `KEWB~`, `AB)".` `A-B-)".`, `AB~é` `A-B-~-é`, `USKN~` `U-S-KN~`, `BCD!5` `B-C-D-!5`, `BCD"5` `B-C-D"5` and `BCD'5` `B-C-D'5`, while `ABCD~` stays as it is. A word in capitals also takes the letters after its signs when these are `~`, `$`, `@`, `&`, `^`, `_`, `=`, `{`, `}`, `<`, `>`, `|`, `%` or a backquote, and then letters and these signs, all counting in its length: `AB~x` is `A-B-~-x`, `AB~a` `A-B-~-aye`, `AB~S` `A-B-~-S`, `NY~a` `NY-~a`, `IO~x` `eye-O-~x`, `IO~xy` `eye-oh-~xy`, `AB~x5` `A-B-~-x5` and `AB~5x` `A-B-~5x`, while `AB~xy`, `AB~~x` and `AB~x~` stay as they are and `AB+x` is `A-B + x`. The letter rules read one with small letters as the word in capitals they make, standing in no run, in the word's own case as for a word with a small head (section 3.12): `BCD AB~x BCD` is `BCD A-B-~-x BCD`, while `BCD AB~X BCD` stays as it is. A word whose capitals start with five or more takes any letters after its signs, and its signs may hold a dot: `EDULC~dog` is `ed-yoo-LC~dog`, `LASRB.~` `L-A-S-R-B-.-~` and `LASRB$5x` `L-A-S-R-B-$5x`. So does a case piece or a word with a small head of any length (sections 3.11 and 3.12), when no quote, `'` or `*` follows its capitals: `USe~dog` is `U-S-e~dog`, `LAn~.~` `L-A-n-~-.-~` and `iOS~dog` `iO-S~dog`. A word is **lowered** when it has no opening marks, the first word step 9 of section 3.4 leaves of it has nothing after its letters or a character that is no letter and then anything, and its letters, two or more of them, capitals or small letters, start with `Q`, `X` or `Z` followed by a consonant, but not `ZW` in any case, and not when they, in capitals, with the digits, the characters from `@` to `~` and those from 128 up of the rest of that first word, are `C`, `D`, `I`, `L`, `M`, `V` and `X` only, nor when they, in capitals, are one of `respelled` and it has no closing marks. ### 3.11 Changes of case

Before section 3.4 changes a paragraph for its spacing (step 1 on), it splits the paragraph's words at their **changes of case**. ## 4. Where the port differs

- **A month of more than three characters.** A date whose month has a number of 1 to 12 but more than three characters, such as `0001/2/3` or `65537/1/1`, doesn't give the original a usable result: it can crash. The port leaves the line as it is.
- **Very long words and lines.** Where a fraction's token runs to about 60 characters, or the conversion makes a line grow to about 720 characters, the original's output doesn't follow section 2's rules. The port applies them all the same. Of the texts checked, 2,517 were such texts.
- **Paragraphs near the limit.** In a paragraph of about 4,500 characters or more, the original writes a lone dot before a word that isn't capitalized with two spaces after it in some texts and one in others, as the texts read before it leave it: `world. .  into` or `world. . into`. In one text it widens a gap before a capitalized word that section 3.3 leaves as it is. The port applies section 3 all the same. Of the texts checked, 21 were such texts.
- **List words beside a dot and a slash.** A list word with a dot and a comma before a slash, a plain one with a dot before a word of `!` and `?` that ends in a slash, and `may` with two dots before a slash are spaced by the original otherwise than section 3 has it: `my acct.,/ is here` is `my account., / is here`, `my gui. !/ is here` `my gooey.! / is here` and `my may../ x is here` `my May . / x is here`. The port applies section 3 all the same (`my account.,  / is here`, `my gooey.!  / is here` and `my May. x is here`). Of the texts checked, 25 were such texts.
- **Control characters.** The port marks what it has read in a paragraph of the address and e-mail contexts with control characters (0 to 10, 14 and 15). A paragraph with the characters 1, 2, 4 to 8, 14 or 15, which the original writes as they are, can be written otherwise by the port.
- **Output size.** TextData gives the pre-formatter an output buffer of the text's length and 4,001 bytes, at least 13,500. The port has no such limit.
